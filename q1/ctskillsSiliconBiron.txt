Scenario

The PSHS school canteen is small and often gets crowded during lunch break. Students line up to buy food, but the process is slow because:

Some students take too long to decide what to order.
The cashier has to manually calculate totals and give change.
There is no system to track which food items are running out.
Your group’s task is to decompose this problem into smaller, manageable parts that could be solved with computational thinking (CT) Skills.

Step 1: Identify the Big Problem

Main Problem: The PSHS canteen experiences severe congestion, slow transaction times, and operational inefficiency during peak lunch hours due to manual ordering, payment processing, and inventory tracking.

Step 2: Identify three to four Sub-Problems
Please list possible sub-problems:

1. Ordering delay, students take a long time to decide on their orders while standing at the counter.

2. Payment processing bottleneck, manual calculation of order totals and change distribution by the cashier slows down transactions.

3. Inventory tracking deficit, lack of real-time inventory monitoring leads to unexpected stockouts and order cancellations at the counter.

4. Queue management failure, high volume of students gathering unstructuredly leads to crowding and disorganization.

Step 3: Define Computational Thinking Approaches
For each sub-problem, apply CT skills:

1. 
Sub-Problem: Ordering Delay

CT Skill: Abstraction

Example Solution: Display an updated digital menu board or pre-ordering mobile interface showing available items, prices, and combos.

2. 
Sub-Problem: Payment processing bottleneck

CT Skill: Algorithm design

Example Solution: Implement an automated Point of Sale (POS) system or barcode/RFID student card scanner that automatically sums costs, accepts digital payments, and calculates precise change.

3. 
Sub-Problem: Inventory tracking deficit

CT Skill: Pattern recognition

Example Solution: Analyze historical sales data to predict popular food items during specific days/times and auto-deduct stock upon purchase, triggering alerts when threshold levels are reached.

4. 
Sub-Problem: Queue management failure

CT Skill: Decomposition

Example Solution: Break the single main line into distinct functional pipelines: pre-order/express pickup, regular ordering, and self-service checkout zones.

 Step 4: Draw a flowchart or write a pseudocode for the identified sub-problem

START
    INPUT TotalAmount
    INPUT AmountPaid

    IF AmountPaid < TotalAmount THEN
        PRINT "Insufficient funds. Please provide remaining payment."
        WHILE AmountPaid < TotalAmount
            INPUT AdditionalPayment
            AmountPaid = AmountPaid + AdditionalPayment
        ENDWHILE
    ENDIF

    ChangeToReturn = AmountPaid - TotalAmount

    IF ChangeToReturn > 0 THEN
        PRINT "Transaction Successful. Change due: " + ChangeToReturn
    ELSE
        PRINT "Transaction Successful. Exact amount received."
    ENDIF

    UPDATE InventoryStatus
    PRINT Receipt
END
